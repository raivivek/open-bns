@CHAPTER IV
@CHAPTER-TITLE OF ABETMENT, CRIMINAL CONSPIRACY AND ATTEMPT
@HEADING of abetment
@SECTION 45 | Abetment of a thing.
P1| A person abets the doing of a thing, who—
P2| (a) instigates any person to do that thing; or
P2| (b) engages with one or more other person or persons in any conspiracy for the doing of that thing, if an act or illegal omission takes place in pursuance of that conspiracy, and in order to the doing of that thing; or
P2| (c) intentionally aids, by any act or illegal omission, the doing of that thing.
P1| Explanation 1.—A person who, by wilful misrepresentation, or by wilful concealment of a material fact which he is bound to disclose, voluntarily causes or procures, or attempts to cause or procure, a thing to be done, is said to instigate the doing of that thing.
@ILLUSTRATION Illustration.
P1| A, a public officer, is authorised by a warrant from a Court to apprehend Z. B, knowing that fact and also that C is not Z, wilfully represents to A that C is Z, and thereby intentionally causes A to apprehend C. Here B abets by instigation the apprehension of C.
P1| Explanation 2.—Whoever, either prior to or at the time of the commission of an act, does anything in order to facilitate the commission of that act, and thereby facilitates the commission thereof, is said to aid the doing of that act.
@SECTION 46 | Abettor.
P1| A person abets an offence, who abets either the commission of an offence, or the commission of an act which would be an offence, if committed by a person capable by law of committing an offence with the same intention or knowledge as that of the abettor.
P1| Explanation 1.—The abetment of the illegal omission of an act may amount to an offence although the abettor may not himself be bound to do that act.
P1| Explanation 2.—To constitute the offence of abetment it is not necessary that the act abetted should be committed, or that the effect requisite to constitute the offence should be caused.
@ILLUSTRATION Illustrations.
P1| (a) A instigates B to murder C. B refuses to do so. A is guilty of abetting B to commit murder.
P1| (b) A instigates B to murder D. B in pursuance of the instigation stabs D. D recovers from the wound. A is guilty of instigating B to commit murder.
P1| Explanation 3.—It is not necessary that the person abetted should be capable by law of committing an offence, or that he should have the same guilty intention or knowledge as that of the abettor, or any guilty intention or knowledge.
